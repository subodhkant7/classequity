# ClassEquity product story

Every child belongs on the trip. Every payment is accounted for.

A class trip begins with possibility. Organizing it can mean reconciling registration spreadsheets, changing group prices, private requests for help and payment screenshots. The same people trying to include everyone also need an honest picture of the money.

ClassEquity gives that work a shared structure. A school reviews a vendor quotation, communicates approved information and invites families. Parents see an estimate and consent to a ceiling. More participation can unlock a lower tier; the school freezes the price only within consent. Assistance remains private, while voluntary sponsors see permitted aggregate needs. Finance sees verified captures, fees, refunds and unresolved outcomes rather than treating promises as cash.

The differentiator is the connection between inclusion and financial correctness. Five fully assisted seats still cost five vendor seats. A sponsor commitment is not money. A completed payment can have unresolved fee accounting. A refunded payment can retain a fee. The product makes these distinctions understandable without exposing recipients.

Gemini helps extract quotations, answer approved-information questions in English/Hindi and explain permitted financial aggregates. Humans review, consent and authorize. The financial engine uses integer cents; AI cannot mutate payments or decide support eligibility. AG Studio presents real editable analytical views.

The public experience starts with a browser-local six-chapter tour. It uses fictional, immutable fixtures and isolated component state. Its separately labeled recorded $1 PayPal sandbox example has one verified capture, a duplicate request that retained one capture, a full $1 refund and a $0.54 retained fee, leaving −$0.54 net. No visitor creates a transaction through the tour.

This is a hackathon implementation, not a claim of adoption, school partnerships, production security certification, escrow or charitable processing. Public HTTPS/webhook delivery and live WhatsApp delivery remain unverified. Hosted PostgreSQL exists; the web application is not deployed.
